## Strongest Behaviors
- **Technical-Creative Integration:** Bridges tool development with visual output; equally comfortable optimizing workflows as crafting final aesthetics.
- **Deep Focus & System Building:** Excels at building procedural systems, tool pipelines, and foundational technical infrastructure that enables creative work.
- **Structured Autonomy:** Works best with clear briefs and regular feedback loops, then self-directs execution without needing micromanagement.
- **Small Team Collaboration:** Natural fit in tight crews; communicates clearly across technical and creative disciplines.

## How You Work Best
- **Project structure:** Longer-form projects with time to develop systems properly (weeks/months, not days). Comfortable with contract/project-based structures.
- **Feedback loops:** Clear upfront direction with regular check-ins; then autonomous execution. Avoid fully open-ended briefs or heavy micromanagement.
- **Team size:** Small crews (2-8 people) preferred. Large departments feel diffuse.
- **Scope variety:** Variety *across projects* matters more than minute-by-minute variety; boredom comes from repetitive work, not from focused effort on one complex system.
- **Creative-technical balance:** Work that combines problem-solving, tool-building, and visual/aesthetic output. Pure admin or non-creative work is draining.

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Preference for structure over ambiguity:** Works best with clear briefs; fully open-ended exploration without direction can feel unmoored. Frame as: "I deliver best when project goals are well-defined—I prefer clarifying upfront over iterating widely."
- **Focus on depth over breadth:** Would rather spend weeks mastering one system than juggling five partial projects. Frame as: "I'm at my best when I can focus deeply on a problem and see it through to polished completion."

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "Building systems", "tool development", "pipeline", "procedural", "technical lead"
- "Longer-form projects", "deep work", "collaborative", "creative + technical", "small team"
- "Clear direction", "iterative feedback", "autonomous", "mentorship"
- "Blender/Unreal/motion/VFX" combined with "technical"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker, but research further):
- "Very fast-paced", "constantly switching tasks", "startup chaos"
- "Fully ambiguous briefs" or "open-ended exploration with minimal direction"
- "Large team dynamics", "siloed departments"
- Pure admin, data entry, or non-creative repetitive work

## Management Style Preferences
- **Works well with:** Clear goal-setting upfront, then trust to execute; regular (weekly or bi-weekly) feedback check-ins; mentorship on technical direction; autonomy over daily methods.
- **Doesn't work well with:** Micromanagement, constantly-shifting requirements, vague briefs with no direction, isolated work without feedback, large committee sign-offs.

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Emphasize "systems thinking", "collaborative problem-solving", "taking ownership from brief to completion". Reference specific examples of procedural workflows or tool development.
- **CV:** Lead with technical achievements (tools, pipelines, optimizations) paired with visual/creative output (what it was used for, impact). Show both sides.
- **Interviews:** Use STAR examples that show: taking a vague problem and clarifying it, building a system from scratch, mentoring or leading a small team, iterating with feedback.
- **Don't overstate:** Avoid claiming "I thrive in chaos" or "I'm great at total ambiguity"—be honest that you work best with clear direction. This isn't a weakness; it's how you deliver polished work.
